# Teach Me to Live

## Register

brand

The site is the product. There is no app to log into here. Every page is identity,
tone, and a way to reach the work. Design carries the whole message.

## What This Is

Teach Me To Live LLC is a one-person software studio in Colorado. It builds
intentional, minimalist tools for learning. The flagship is ProtoQuiz, an EMS
training app built from a provider's own local protocols. Other work: the Teach
Me To Live iOS app (anonymous wisdom that expires monthly) and SleepMedic.

The site at teachmetolive.com is the studio's front door and its statement of
taste. It is not a funnel. It does not sell. It signals that the person behind
these tools reads, thinks, and cares about craft.

## The Homepage Is a Reading Room

Confirmed and non-negotiable: the homepage is a rotating literary quote and
nothing else. Socrates first, then a shuffled pool of ~40 passages from Seneca,
Dostoevsky, Faulkner, Frankl, Dickinson, Pressfield, Goggins, Tesla. Crossfade
between them. Long quotes get more time on screen.

The quote IS the hero. ProtoQuiz gets one line at the bottom. This inversion is
deliberate: a studio that leads with Faulkner instead of a signup button is
making a claim about itself.

Do not add a value proposition above the quote. Do not add a CTA button to the
hero. Do not turn the homepage into a landing page.

## Users

- **EMS providers and paramedics** who found ProtoQuiz and want to know who made
  it. They are tired, smart, and allergic to marketing language. They serve
  people at their worst moments.
- **People who read.** The quote pool is the filter. Someone who stops for the
  Seneca passage is the audience.
- **Prospective partners and agencies** doing due diligence before trusting a
  small studio with their protocols. The site must read as legitimate, not as a
  weekend project.

## Tone

Quiet confidence. Literary without being precious. Plain-language legal pages.
Never salesy, never hype, never startup-voice. The writing on the site should
sound like the person who chose those quotes.

## Brand

- Near-black ground. Established and liked; keep it dark.
- Serif for the quote (currently Iowan Old Style / Palatino stack), quiet
  uppercase micro-caps for chrome and attribution.
- The emblem is a small square mark, used at 56px above the quote. Restraint.

## Strategic Principles

1. **The quote is the product.** Every design decision serves reading it.
2. **Restraint is the signal.** Emptiness is intentional, not unfinished.
3. **Nothing shouts.** No badges, no gradients-as-personality, no hero metrics.
4. **The chrome recedes.** Nav, footer, and attribution stay under the content.
5. **Legitimacy through craft.** An agency evaluating ProtoQuiz should see a
   studio that finishes things.

## Anti-References

- SaaS landing pages. No feature grids, no pricing tiers, no "Trusted by".
- Startup dark mode: purple-to-blue gradients, glowing cards, neon accents.
- Medium / Substack literary-blog defaults.
- Quote-app aesthetics: script fonts, sunset photos, Instagram-tile framing.
- Anything that reads as a template with the logo swapped.

## Current State

Live via GitHub Pages at teachmetolive.com. Five pages: index (Reading Room),
about, projects, contact, plus privacy and terms. Single hand-written stylesheet.
No build step, no framework, no dependencies. Keep it that way.

## The Brief

The concept and the near-black direction are both approved and staying. The gap
is execution: the site is currently correct but not beautiful. It should feel
made rather than assembled.
